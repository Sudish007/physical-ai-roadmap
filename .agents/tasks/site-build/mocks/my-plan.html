Month by month, 10 h plan §15.5

Months 1–9 are common. From month 10 the table shows Track B wording as the default; the A/C replacements are in §14.3.

  • 1 · W1–4 · 40 h
    Entry diagnostic; Python numerical refresh; units, plots, seeds, environment capture; start lab notes. Deliverable: Diagnostic memo; reproducible noisy-line and first-order-system experiment; gap list. Notebook or script, no framework.
  • 2 · W5–8 · 40 h
    Selected linear algebra, derivatives/integrals, numerical integration, probability, force/torque, inertia (M0, MML chs. 2–6). Deliverable: 2-D transform and point-mass simulation with units and tests. Skip proofs beyond what the calculation needs.
  • 3 · W9–13 · 50 h
    ODE intuition, feedback, PID, train/validation/test discipline, first paper memos (M1 selections). Deliverable: Controlled first/second-order plant; Q1 gate report; one two-page paper critique. One controller, one disturbance.
  • 4 · W14–17 · 40 h
    Rigid transforms, frames, rotations, forward kinematics, calibration concepts (M2 begins). Deliverable: Transform library; frame diagram; synthetic calibration-error experiment. Planar plus minimal 3-D.
  • 5 · W18–21 · 40 h
    Rigid-body dynamics, actuator/sensor models, PID tuning, optimization basics (M2). Deliverable: One bounded plant, such as a planar quadrotor or simple arm, with tracking and logs (Rung 1). PID only.
  • 6 · W22–26 · 50 h
    Electronics literacy without hardware: V/I/P, PWM, UART/I²C/SPI, IMU and camera timing, batteries, motors/ESCs, aliasing (M4 literacy); stress the controller. Deliverable: Virtual sensor/actuator interface spec; mass/latency/noise disturbance report; Q2 gate. Datasheets and simulated sensors only.
  • 7 · W27–30 · 40 h
    Conditional probability, Gaussian models, Bayes filtering, Kalman filter (Rung 2; Barfoot early chapters; M3 begins). Deliverable: Seeded estimation experiment with RMSE, residual, and interval-coverage plots. Linear Kalman filter.
  • 8 · W31–34 · 40 h
    Search- and sampling-based planning, collision checking, trajectory representation (LaValle selections; M3). Deliverable: A*/RRT planner over fixed seeded maps; failure taxonomy. One planner and one baseline.
  • 9 · W35–39 · 50 h
    ROS 2 concepts, timestamps, transforms, recording/replay, latency, deterministic evaluation (M5; Rung 3); complete the §14 decision memo. Deliverable: Integrated simulated sense-estimate-plan loop; replay regenerates the metrics table; Q3 gate and Track A/B/C choice. One process graph, one recording format.
  • 10 · W40–43 · 40 h
    Freeze the stack; PX4 SITL, simulator reset, mission state, logs, emergency-state semantics (Rung 4; use the NPTEL PX4 lectures as just-in-time reference). Deliverable: Scripted nominal takeoff, approach, landing, abort, and reset protocol with complete logs. One airframe, one world.
  • 11 · W44–47 · 40 h
    Small visual landing-pad detector/pose estimator; data split by scene, texture, seed (M6–M7 material). Deliverable: Data card; baseline model; nominal and shifted metrics; latency profile. One compact CPU-capable model.
  • 12 · W48–52 · 50 h
    Join perception to the fixed landing controller; define failure events; collect nominal and disturbed pilot episodes; confirm the month-9 choice (A/C use §14.3 replacements). Deliverable: Closed-loop baseline package; 50 nominal and ≥20 disturbed pilot episodes; Q4 gate. No RL, no hardware.

Personalization questions §4

Answer these in writing during Session 7. Answers stay in this browser; export them with your plan.

  1. 1. Can you, today, compute a least-squares fit and explain eigenvalues, Jacobians, and conditional probability without notes?
    Decision rule

    Fewer than two of three → use the full 30-hour M0 core slice and diagnosed MIT 18.06SC/18.05 exercises inside Q1; do not start ROS before Q1 passes. All three → challenge the M0 outputs, then reassign at most 15 saved hours to Rung 1; never skip the gate.

  2. 2. How comfortable are you with Linux, Python, and C++ (none / use / fluent)?
    Decision rule

    Linux or Python below use → month 1 is tooling practice before any robotics content. C++ none is acceptable until Rung 3; add a small C++ exercise in the 20 h plan.

  3. 3. Have you trained and evaluated a neural network with held-out data and multiple seeds?
    Decision rule

    No → take the 5-hour M6 primer and 15-hour M7 core slice, then spend the already-budgeted Rung 5/6 implementation hours on the baseline. Yes with documented seeds and split hygiene → challenge M6 and move its five hours to M7, without increasing total F hours.

  4. 4. Have you implemented any feedback controller or state estimator?
    Decision rule

    No → keep all Rung 1/2 hours and the 25-hour M3 core slice; use the Bonn lecture within M3, not on top. Yes with a reproducible artifact → challenge at most 10 M3 hours and move them to the same quarter's Rung 1 disturbances.

  5. 5. Degree and career stage (student / early-career engineer / mid-career / other)?
    Decision rule

    Student with access to faculty → pursue the research-master's or RA route at month 18 (§16). Mid-career with income needs → systems/evaluation-engineer route and the 10 h plan.

  6. 6. Have you written a technical report or paper that someone else reviewed?
    Decision rule

    No → monthly two-page memos start in month 1 and the month-15 artifact/reproduction-boundary report is mandatory before the confirmatory thesis experiment.

  7. 7. Hours per week you can protect for 104 weeks (not a best week)?
    Decision rule

    ≤12 → 10 h plan. ≥18 → 20 h plan. Between → follow the 10 h contract; treat surplus as recovery or choose one 20 h extension only by logging the extra hours separately. Never claim the 1,040-hour completion if hidden overtime was used.

  8. 8. Budget in INR over 24 months for hardware and fees?
    Decision rule

    Any budget → Scenario A until Rung 6. Scenarios B and C remain illustrative foreign-goods arithmetic, not orderable Indian budgets, regardless of the nominal INR conversion. Compare written landed Indian quotes with institutional access only after every §12.6 field is complete; no unverified rupee threshold routes a purchase.

  9. 9. Computer: CPU cores, RAM, free SSD, discrete GPU and VRAM?
    Decision rule

    No NVIDIA GPU → choose Track B or C and use the safe-control-gym v1.0.0 artifact study, whose local result is a single-machine smoke observation with a partial manifest; do not call a checkpoint-only run a full-paper reproduction. No Diffusion Policy author run, VLA fine-tuning, or Isaac Sim. OpenVLA's repository reported ≈27 GB GPU memory for LoRA; Isaac Sim's official minimum was RTX 4080/16 GB. Any GPU → first run the exact Track-A pilot and record peak VRAM/runtime; Track A becomes feasible only if that pilot fits.

  10. 10. Do you have access to a lab, makerspace, or mentor who can inspect your work?
    Decision rule

    No → use the three-role mentor board in §16 via reproductions and issues; prefer Track B or C, which need no physical facility. Yes → Rung 7–8 can be scheduled in the 20 h plan.

  11. 11. Do you have a safe indoor enclosure or institutional flight space?
    Decision rule

    No → Rung 8 drone hardware is removed; hardware questions route to a tabletop arm or an institution. Never substitute outdoor flight.

  12. 12. City/state, and can you travel for supervised test days?
    Decision rule

    Near IISc/ARTPARK (Bengaluru), IIIT Hyderabad, IIT Kanpur/Bombay/Madras → investigate seminars and collaboration leads in §14 and §16. Remote → remote testbeds and online reproductions.

  13. 13. Comfort with electronics and mechanics (none / hobby / trained)?
    Decision rule

    None → M4 stays simulation/literacy-only and any bench work is supervised. Trained → replace M4's 10-hour core/5-hour extension with equal-hour extracts from the UT Austin embedded course; do not add its full 72 hours.

  14. 14. Which problem pulls you: manipulation, flight, legged/humanoid, evaluation and safety, scientific robotics?
    Decision rule

    Manipulation → Track A if Q9 and Q10 are favourable. Flight → Track B. Evaluation/safety → Track C. Humanoid → Track A in simulation only (HumanoidBench); humanoid hardware is out of individual scope. Scientific robotics → Track C with the F12 entry experiment.

  15. 15. Theory versus building (prefer derivations / prefer systems / both)?
    Decision rule

    Derivations → Track C and the 5-hour M11 safety/control extract, plus only the 20 h plan's allocated extension. Systems → Track B and the already-budgeted Rungs 3–4.

  16. 16. Career target in five years (industry research engineer / PhD / startup / academic)?
    Decision rule

    PhD → month-18 route decision requires letters and a thesis protocol; publish a reproduction first. Startup → add the IERA/commercialization lens in §18 but do not skip the evidence ladder.

  17. 17. When do you want a first external review or submission?
    Decision rule

    < 12 months → submit a reproduction or negative-result report to a workshop or technical-report venue; do not promise a method paper. 18–24 months → the plan's month-22–23 manuscript.

  18. 18. Any constraints from employer, institution, or law (IP, export, flight, data)?
    Decision rule

    Any → write them into the protocol at month 13 and the ethics checklist in §16; employer IP rules can determine whether artifacts can be released.
